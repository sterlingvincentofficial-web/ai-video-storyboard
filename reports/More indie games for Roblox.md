# Seven Fresh Indie Mechanics Roblox Hasn't Cloned

Beyond the shortlist in the earlier report, the late-October 2026 indie crop holds **seven mechanics with no Roblox version and no close Roblox genre found in web search**. Ranked, they are a two-player physics carry (Fallbearers), a photo-countdown face-matching toy (Picture Day), a stretchy-arm heist (My Arms Are Longer Now), a co-op RC-car puzzle duo (WheelMates), a falling-price auction draft (Veggie Brawl), a crew-a-giant-machine sim (Iron Nest), and, as a strong exception that sits in tier B, a critter break-in sandbox (Dumpster Gang). The three to prototype first are **Wobble Couriers** (from Fallbearers), **Boardwalk Bandits** (from Dumpster Gang, rebuilt around Roblox's steal-and-defend loop) and **Snapface Studio** (from Picture Day). Each is cheap to build, easy to clip and safe for kids, and each can carry a collection loop for Roblox's 28-day retention signal. The pattern behind the list matters as much as the list. Almost every **released** hit from June to October, including Big Walk (1M copies in six days), Shift At Midnight (953k in July), ReStory (500k+) and Cat Mail Co. (236k), already has a Roblox genre neighbour. The open lanes are mostly **unreleased Next Fest demos and jam prototypes**, so the "don't launch a lookalike before the original ships" rule bites hardest exactly where the opportunity is largest. WheelMates and Iron Nest are the exceptions: they are already on sale and still uncloned, which makes their timing the cleanest on the list. Of 23 viable candidates across the three research files, 7 rank as tier A and 16 as tier B, and about 28 more were rejected. Every figure comes from search-engine summaries, because Steam, SteamDB, Roblox and most news sites were blocked. Every "no clone" verdict is web-search-only and must be re-checked in Roblox's own search before any build. Nothing here is legal advice.

The ranking weighs five things: evidence of virality, fit with Roblox's young and social audience, how open the Roblox space is, build cost for a small team, and how well the mechanic can carry a 28-day progression loop. Fit scores (1–5) come from the research notes; where the notes disagreed, both values are shown. Tiers follow the brief. **(A)** means no Roblox version and no close Roblox genre found. **(B)** means no direct clone, but a similar Roblox genre exists. Dumpster Gang is placed in B even though one file called it wide open. ## Tier A pitches borrow the verb and replace everything else

Each pitch keeps the borrowed mechanic and swaps out everything that carries the original's creative signature: title, setting, characters, art direction and UI. It adds a twist the original lacks, plus a reason to return between days 8 and 28. That last part is not optional. Roblox rebuilt Recommended For You around retention in June 2026 ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox)), and its retention window grew from 7 to 28 days, split into D1, D2–7 and D8–28 ([ZehnStudio26](https://zehn-studio26.com/news/recommended-for-you-retention-update/)). A one-session party toy that people try once now gets buried. All names are **placeholder working titles**. Each needs a Roblox search and a USPTO trademark search before use.

**Wobble Couriers** ranks first because its source has the cheapest build on the list, and a physics failure is funny within three seconds with no context. Those are the two traits most tied to clip spread in 2026. Shareable mechanics produce "a 15 to 30 second moment a stranger understands without context" ([Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/indie-games-viral-tiktok)), and the viral games of the summer made progress visible and looked like content audiences already watch ([itch.io blog](https://itch.io/blog/1649609/what-4-viral-indie-games-had-in-common-and-it-wasnt-marketing-budget)). The funeral premise is the original's expression and is also a poor fit for kids, so dropping it improves both legal distance and audience fit. One overlap matters inside this research programme. The earlier report's **Hatchling Hustle** (from Keep It Up!) is also a "protect the fragile object as a team" game. A studio should build one of the two, or merge them so that cakes and eggs share one delivery world, rather than compete with itself.

**Snapface Studio** and **Noodle Grab** both rest on one physical gag: a face pulled out of shape, an arm stretched too far. A one-gag game is the format the 28-day signal punishes, so each pitch puts a collection layer under the gag. Picture Day's evidence is curatorial rather than viral, since it won Mark Brown's personal favourite slot in a 10,500-entry jam ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-jam-2026/results)), and My Arms Are Longer Now has no measured player numbers at all. Both are bets on mechanic quality, not on proven demand. **Backyard RC Rescue** and **Tidewalker Crew** are the only tier-A pitches whose sources are already on sale, so neither can be accused of beating its original to market. Iron Nest has the strongest commercial evidence in tier A (~650k units) but the hardest translation. It is single-player, its fantasy is war, and its ballistics interface is hard for young players. That is why it ranks seventh despite the sales. **Going, Going, Gone!** borrows the most Roblox-native social mechanic in the set. A falling price creates a "who blinks first" moment in front of friends, and that tension is the point. The design must keep real-money purchases entirely out of the auction, or the mechanic turns into pay-to-win in a single patch.

## Tier B pitches must out-design an incumbent

Tier B games have no direct Roblox version, but a Roblox game already serves players who want the same feeling. A twist is not enough to win here; the pitch has to beat the incumbent on some axis. Usually that axis is a player-versus-player layer the incumbent lacks, or a stronger collection loop.

**Boardwalk Bandits** leads tier B, and ranks second overall, because it swaps Dumpster Gang's loop into the most proven retention format on Roblox. Steal-and-defend is the structure behind Steal a Brainrot's reported ~24M-player peak ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)), and a nest you can lose to a rival is a reason to log in on day 20. The seagull reskin also gets around the one real competitor. "Gnomes!" is a co-op burglary against an NPC homeowner ([RobloxDen](https://robloxden.com/game-codes/gnomes)), while Boardwalk Bandits is a flock-versus-flock economy in an outdoor space. Its timing risk is specific: Dumpster Gang launches on **Dec 3** ([Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2026/10/dumpster-gang-launches-december-3)). A three-to-five-week build lands near that date anyway. If it lands first, the game's distance from the original must be unmistakable: birds, not raccoons, and a beach, not a city alley.

The cozy job sims (**Penguin Parcel Post**, **Robot Repair Bay**) come next because their sources have the best hard sales numbers in tier B and the youngest-friendly loops. They rank below the top three because Roblox already has a mail game and a repair game, so each pitch must win on scope rather than novelty. **Trail Signals** and **Shapeshifter Diner** draw on the two biggest commercial hits in the research, Big Walk and Shift At Midnight. Each needs a real redesign, though. Big Walk is built on proximity voice, which a low-quality source says Roblox limits to age-verified users 13 and over ([search summary](https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/news/create-a-roblox-voice-chat)). Shift At Midnight is rated 16+ ([Xbox](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/games/store/Shift-At-Midnight/9N0WDPMXNHWN)) and sits in a doppelganger genre Roblox already covers. Turning its NPC impostors into player impostors is the one change that makes a Roblox version better than both the original and the incumbent.

## Patterns: the open lanes are unreleased, physical and small-bodied

Four patterns cut across the new list. **First, released hits are nearly always tier B.** Of the twelve released June–October hits with a completed Roblox check (counting the rejected Machine Party and Burglin' Gnomes), only WheelMates, Iron Nest and the technically unsuitable Sandustry had no close Roblox genre. Mail sorting, gadget repair, gem cutting, hiking, off-roading, wizard schools, tornado chasing and doppelganger checking were all already present on Roblox. The interpretation: Roblox's existing catalog already covers most *job-sim and genre-sim* premises, and new lanes come from **novel verbs** (carrying with a partner, stretching, contorting, falling-price bidding) rather than novel jobs. Those verbs mostly live in demos and jam games that haven't shipped, which is why the timing caveat in the final section matters so much.

**Second, "be a small or silly body" is the dominant hook.** Raccoons, tiny gnomes, a brick phone, RC cars, cats and a stretchy arm all turn the player character into the joke. That suits Roblox, where avatar cosmetics carry monetization, and it gives every pitch an obvious cosmetic layer. **Third, the communication-puzzle games depend on voice.** Big Walk, Meltdown Crew and DYST all build their fun on talking, and that is the most fragile assumption for a young Roblox audience. Each pitch here replaces voice with pings, flags, preset callouts or split information, and those UI choices are where the original design work happens. **Fourth, many hits need a theme transplant to be kid-safe.** Bodies (No Body No Problem!, We Are So Cooked, Fallbearers), amputation toys (HANDS OVER), war artillery (Iron Nest) and 16+ doppelganger horror (Shift At Midnight) all need new fiction. That is helpful, because the reskin that makes a game kid-safe also creates the legal distance from the original.

The clone environment explains why speed still matters but cannot be the plan. Next Fest October 2026 ran a record ~7,100–8,000 demos and was dominated by co-op "friendslop" ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/10/21/how-sloppy-is-the-october-2026-steam-next-fest/); [AccountShark](https://accountshark.net/blog/steam-next-fest-october-2026-most-played-demos-what-to-play-before-it-ends)). The Needle in a Haystack clip drew ~48M views and at least 14 Steam clones, which then cloned each other ([ixbt.games](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/08/24/indi-razrabotcik-pokazal-simuliator-poiska-igolki-v-stoge-sena-i-sobral-48-millionov-prosmotrov.html); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/devs-behind-needle-in-a-haystack-clone-complain-their-clone-has-been-cloned-by-vibe-coder-2000747284)). Roblox imitators of Meccha Chameleon reportedly drew more players than the original ([GosuGamers](https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/78805-roblox-clones-of-meccha-chameleon-and-other-steam-hits-are-soaring-in-popularity)). Any tier-A lane on this list will probably close within weeks of its source's next spike. Dumpster Gang's Dec 3 launch and HANDS OVER's possible Oct 30 Early Access are the nearest spikes.

## Prototype Wobble Couriers, Boardwalk Bandits and Snapface Studio first

The top three were chosen as a portfolio. Each tests a different retention engine, all three are cheap enough to prototype in a few weeks (my estimate), and none leans on the source's branding to get noticed.

**Wobble Couriers (from Fallbearers)** is the purest open lane. It has no Roblox version and no close genre in web search, a fit score of 5 in the notes, Kotaku's lone Next Fest endorsement ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/mix-coffin-flop-with-qwop-and-youve-got-a-game-thats-at-the-top-of-my-steam-next-fest-list-2000746058)), and a physics core that a small team can build quickly. Its retention engine is **collection and routes**: a delivery book of cargo types and districts that open up over time. Its risk is that the source shows editorial buzz rather than player numbers, so the prototype should test whether a duo carry stays funny past the fifth run. If the studio has already started the earlier report's Hatchling Hustle, merge the two concepts rather than build both.

**Boardwalk Bandits (from Dumpster Gang)** is tier B but makes the top three on evidence and loop. Its source was a Valve top-20 Next Fest demo ([GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/news/here-are-the-20-most-played-demos-from-octobers-steam-next-fest/)), animal-mischief co-op scored 5 for fit in both notes files, and its retention engine is **steal-and-defend**, the most proven long loop on Roblox ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). The cost is a direct competitor ("Gnomes!") and an original due on Dec 3. The prototype has to prove that a flock-versus-flock nest economy feels different from a burglary against an NPC homeowner. If it doesn't, drop it.

**Snapface Studio (from Picture Day)** is the cheapest experiment and the most kid-safe. A face-matching round needs almost no level art, and lobby voting gives it endless replay. Its retention engine is **cosmetic collection**, with face parts, species and a photo album, which is also where its revenue comes from. Its source is a free jam game with curator recognition rather than market proof ([GMTK Substack](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game-50b)), so it is a quick bet on mechanic quality: build it in days, run it with a few lobbies, and keep it only if players replay it.

Two runners-up deserve a slot if any of the three fails. **Backyard RC Rescue** has the cleanest timing on the list, since WheelMates shipped in September with no Roblox analogue found, but puzzle rooms consume content quickly. **Noodle Grab** has the most clippable verb, but its source has the thinnest evidence.

## Blocked sources, web-only clone checks and timing rules limit every verdict

**Data gaps.** All three research passes ran into the same wall. WebFetch failed on DNS, and the proxy returned 403 for Steam, SteamDB, SteamSpy, Roblox (including its search API and DevForum), itch.io, Reddit, YouTube, X, TikTok, GameDiscoverCo pages and several news sites. **Every figure above comes from search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not full reads**, and the shared search budget ran out before several checks were finished. Unit figures conflict in places. Shift At Midnight shows 953k from GameDiscoverCo against ~403k from a review-count estimate ([SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/tools/revenue-calculator/3722330)), and tracker estimates from Raijin and SteamSpy generally run 2–3x below GameDiscoverCo. Developers are unknown for Picture Day, Grr Grr Gnomes, Kick the Piñata!, Inside Job, Solarpunk and Pass the Fear. Iron Nest's developer names conflict. Release dates conflict for HANDS OVER (Oct 30, 2026 or 2028), Flipping Is Hard and WheelMates (Sept 1 or 7). No view counts were found for Dumpster Gang, Grr Grr Gnomes, Destroy Any Website or My Arms Are Longer Now. **Roblox clone checks were web-search-only.** "None found" means no Roblox experience appeared in web results, and small or new experiences are often not indexed. Before building any of these, search Roblox itself for the generic mechanic keywords (carry, cake, seagull, face match, stretch arm, RC car, auction) and re-check the incumbent games named above. Snaptic's check was never completed, and Pass the Fear, Solarpunk, Voidling Bound, Inside Job and Loftia were never checked at all.

**Timing caveats.** Most tier-A sources are unreleased: Fallbearers has no date, My Arms Are Longer Now has no date, and Dumpster Gang ships Dec 3. Launching a lookalike before an original ships is the move that has drawn the loudest backlash, as with PEAK's developer calling a Roblox copy "slop" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/peak-dev-would-rather-you-pirate-peak-than-play-a-microtransaction-roblox-slop-ripoff/)) and with the clone waves documented by [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/09/17/the-attack-of-the-roblox-clones/). The answer is distance, not just delay. A seagull boardwalk raid or a cake-delivery duo reads as a new genre entry. A raccoon dumpster game or a coffin carry would read as a copy whenever it launched. If a pitch cannot be made clearly distinct, wait until the original has shipped.

**Legal caveats.** Take mechanics, not expression. US courts have treated game rules as unprotectable while protecting art, characters and look-and-feel. *DaVinci Editrice v. Ziko Games* found no infringement where identical rules were re-themed ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/game-mechanics-arent-protected-copyright-according-texas-court-ruling)), and *Tetris v. Xio* found infringement where the visual design was copied ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc.)). Roblox's IP guidelines let rights holders target copied expression "even if it has been modified, including by AI tools" ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/ip-guidelines)), and Rights Manager accepts claims from holders with no Roblox presence ([Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/rights-manager)). Each "Do not reuse" column above is the minimum list to avoid. Titles carry separate trademark risk, so clear every placeholder name before use. This report summarizes public commentary and platform policy for planning. **It is not legal advice.** Get a lawyer's view before launching anything close to an existing game.

## Conclusion

The second sweep changes the shape of the opportunity. The first report found open windows in a few viral demos. This one shows that **Roblox has already absorbed most indie premises**: job sims, genre sims and cleanup toys all have an incumbent within weeks or already did. What stays open are **verbs** that no Roblox game yet treats as its core, such as carrying with a partner, stretching, contorting your face and bidding against a falling price. A small developer's advantage lies in building a long loop around one of those verbs, not in being first to a premise. Roblox's 28-day retention weighting rewards the same thing, because a verb with a collection loop around it keeps players coming back.

The list also gives an outreach angle. The two tier-A sources already on sale, WheelMates and Iron Nest, are uncloned, commercially proven and come from small teams. An official, revenue-shared Roblox adaptation, the route Bombanana! has already taken with FXFX Studio ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/steam-hit-bombanana-becomes-first-steam-native-party-game-with-an-official-roblox-adaptation/)), is a realistic conversation for either. It would turn the riskiest part of this work, borrowing someone else's idea, into a partnership.
