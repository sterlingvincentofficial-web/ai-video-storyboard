# Remix October's Indie Hits Into Original Roblox Games

The best open opportunities for a small Roblox developer in late October 2026 are not the games that already set off clone waves. They are four mechanics from viral indie games, mostly unreleased, for which web search found **no Roblox clone**. Worst Class Ever has an asymmetric loop: hide among NPCs and pull pranks while one player hunts you. Keep It Up! is co-op physics where you can't let an object touch the floor. Haunted Paws is cozy co-op horror with collectible pets. Fart Together has a team of players steering one shared vehicle. Each pairs real virality with a cheap Roblox build. Worst Class Ever topped Valve's Next Fest list with 300,000+ demo players, Keep It Up! claims 500,000 demo downloads, and Haunted Paws is close to 1M wishlists. Escape the Walls itself is closed. It had seven or more Roblox clones within five days, on top of a wall-punching genre that already claimed 150M+ visits. Speed is no longer the bar to clear, because clones now appear about a day after a reveal. Retention is. Since June 2026, Roblox's recommendations weight 28-day retention. Crossover clones have historically peaked around **50K–84K concurrent players**, while Roblox-native games built on a long progression loop reach **1M+**. The winning recipe is to take the mechanic, rebuild it around a new setting and a Roblox-native long loop (collection, base-building, progression), and ship it with a name, art and UI that share nothing with the original. US courts treat game mechanics as unprotectable and the art, characters and look-and-feel as protected. Roblox removes games only when a rights holder files a claim. But one valid claim takes down the whole experience, and press backlash (PEAK, Escape the Walls) arrives much faster than any lawyer. One caveat runs through everything below: the research could not open Roblox, SteamDB, Reddit or most news sites, so every figure comes from search summaries, and "no clone found" means none turned up in web search. This report is not legal advice.

For Escape the Walls and the needle-in-a-haystack concept, the gap between viral moment and Roblox copy was **hours to about a day**, so any window marked "open" will probably close within days of that game's next viral spike. **Sneak Squad leads** because its source has the strongest current evidence of any open window and its loop is natively Roblox. Rounds are short and asymmetric (1 versus many), NPC crowds are cheap to build, and the setup streams well. Outlets already call Worst Class Ever "a new asymmetrical friendslop game" ([se7en.ws](https://se7en.ws/the-20-most-played-steam-next-fest-demos-include-mischievous-students-and-farting-hippos/?lang=en)), a genre the press now tracks as a named trend after How to Fish's 370,901-player peak ([Player.One](https://www.player.one/how-fish-steams-newest-viral-hit-why-are-chaotic-co-op-games-taking-over-163910); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25105/how-to-fish-sells-1-million-copies-on-steam-in-two-days)). The setting change matters more than it looks. A classroom with a teacher-hunter is Worst Class Ever's signature, while a sleepover with a counselor is a different fiction built on the same hide-in-a-crowd rules. That is exactly the re-skin that has held up legally (see the legal section). The disguise-copy twist and the "Busted!" budget add strategy the original doesn't advertise. Its source also has the shortest life as an open window: the full game is due in Q4 2026, and a generic school-prank game may already exist on Roblox under a name web search can't surface.

**Hatchling Hustle** turns a one-session physics party game into a collection game. That matters because a balloon-keep-up game with no meta-progression is precisely the "fun once" format that 28-day retention penalizes. Eggs are not arbitrary either. Steal An Egg sat in Roblox's top three by live players on Oct 25, 2026 and is running Halloween live events ([Rokoala](https://rokoala.com/roblox-player-count/); [Bloxron](https://bloxron.com/steal-an-egg/stealing)), so the theme rides an existing Roblox trend keyword, the way "[BACKROOMS] Chameleon!" did ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/more-people-are-playing-meccha-chameleon-knock-offs-than-the-original-hit-steam-game-as-the-roblox-clone-machine-goes-into-overdrive-2000715371)). Avoid Hitbox's "FriendSLAP" label, which is its own marketing term ([GamesPress](https://www.gamespress.com/New-FriendSLAP-Title-Keep-It-Up-surpasses-500000-demo-downloads)).

**Nightlight Critters** ranks third on fit with Roblox trends, not on its source's virality. Survival was the fastest-growing Roblox genre in early October (+13% week over week, 2.9M live players), second only to simulation's 5.1M ([Statroblox](https://statroblox.com/guides/most-popular-roblox-genres)). Collectible creatures are the most proven way to monetize cosmetics. Haunted Paws's roughly 1M wishlists show that cozy co-op horror with pets resonates ([PC Games](https://www.pcgames.de/Spiele-Thema-239104/News/Haunted-Paws-Koop-Horror-Welpen-Steam-Wishlist-1552314/)). The pitch deliberately swaps puppies rescuing an owner for small pets defending a bedroom, so nothing in its story or characters overlaps with the original. **Couch Rocket Rally** is the cheapest build on the list and suits kids' slapstick humor. It ranks fourth only because Fart Together's virality evidence is thin and its Next Fest rank conflicts across outlets.

**The second tier carries more build cost or more theme risk.** Dear Passengers has the biggest awareness signal on the board (1.5M wishlists, organic, no demo) and no playable product, which is the exact gap clones exploit. But a plane interior full of task stations is a heavy build for a small team. Its trailer also drew objections over Epstein- and Trump-like satire, which must not carry over ([dev.ua](https://dev.ua/en/news/1-billion-dear-passengers-1784294360)). Probably Stolen launches Oct 28 ([PlayNews](https://www.playnews.gg/en/announcements/probably-stolen-cyberpunk-shopkeeper-sim-launches-on-steam-october-28-2026)), so a multiplayer reinterpretation would arrive after the original ships, which is the cleanest timing on the list. The smuggling theme has to be sanitized (candy instead of contraband), and turning a single-player game into social deduction is real design work. Invokyr's dice-board structure maps neatly onto party rounds but needs a strong long loop of its own. **Tunnel Raiders is the only way back into the crowded incremental space.** The punch-and-upgrade loop is Roblox's most proven simulator template. Punch Wall Simulator has a claimed 150M+ visits since 2023 ([Ruben on X](https://x.com/rdominguezibar/status/2110794822550474870)). The pitch survives only because it merges that loop with steal-and-defend, the genre behind Steal a Brainrot's reported ~24M-player peak ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)), and changes the competition from solo escape to team raids.

**Avoid five things outright.** The first is anything needle-and-haystack. One developer counted 14 Roblox copies within 48 hours ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/man-teases-most-infuriating-game-ever-roblox-vultures-immediately-steal-his-idea-2000802874)), and the leading Roblox version has a 57M–67M visit lead ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/10756011174/clans-search-for-the-needle)). The second is paint-to-hide hide-and-seek, which Meccha Chameleon clones dominate at up to ~50K CCU ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/more-people-are-playing-meccha-chameleon-knock-offs-than-the-original-hit-steam-game-as-the-roblox-clone-machine-goes-into-overdrive-2000715371)). The third is Bombanana!, which already has an **official** Roblox version with 15M+ visits ([Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/?p=409945)). The fourth is a straight fishing game, where Roblox's Fisch is entrenched ([Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/16732694052/Fisch)). The fifth is mature themes such as Scam With Your Friends' phone-scam call center or Hood Warfare 2's shooter, both poor fits for a young audience. Avoid designs that depend on voice chat too: that is my judgment, because voice access for minors on Roblox could not be verified.

## Crossover clones plateau around 50K–84K CCU unless they add a long loop

The case record shows that Roblox versions of Steam hits often beat the originals on live players. They rarely become platform-level hits.

The last row changes how to read this whole brief. **"As viral as the Escape the Walls clones" is an unmeasured target.** The story that went viral was the *cloning*, carried by Game*Spark's quote from a clone maker who said "Claude did everything for me, I just sat in my chair and watched" ([Game*Spark on X](https://x.com/gamespark/status/2111011804524793955)). No outlet reported a player count for any clone. The benchmarks we can actually measure are the Meccha Chameleon clones and Clean the Library. Both are solid mid-tier Roblox hits, and both are far from the front page. One Find the Needle clone reportedly earned $100,000 in a month before the original launched, but that is a single unverified report ([Techora](https://techora.ru/news/roblox-klon-needle-in-a-haystack-2026-09-28)).

**Speed to market has collapsed, and AI tools are why.** The lag from Steam hit to Roblox copy went from weeks (Lethal Company) to about two weeks (PEAK in 2025), to 26 clones in three weeks (Meccha Chameleon), to less than 25 hours for Escape the Walls, whose Roblox copy went live before its Steam page did ([Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2026/10/19/192648)). Roblox-native hits show the same compression: Grow a Garden was reportedly built "in a few days" by a teenager ([NBC/AP](https://www.nbcdfw.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/virtual-gardens-viral-roblox-game-created-teenager/3897336/)), and 99 Nights in the Forest came from a three-person team ([Pryor Cashman](https://www.pryorcashman.com/news/pryor-cashman-client-grandmas-favourite-games-99-nights-in-the-forest-headed-to-the-big-screen)). A small developer cannot out-run vibe coders who ship in a day. Being first is now table stakes, not an edge.

**The ceiling belongs to games that borrow loosely and build long loops.** Dead Rails, a Wild West train-survival game, peaked at 1,476,198 concurrent players ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/116495829188952)), and GameAnalytics credits its "familiar gameplay with fresh twists" ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/dead-rails-and-the-hit-makers-formula)). 99 Nights hit 14.2M CCU ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/survival-crafting/with-a-peak-player-count-of-14-2-million-99-nights-in-the-forest-has-an-audience-other-multiplayer-games-would-kill-for-to-find-these-behemoth-playerbases-you-need-to-be-on-a-platform-like-roblox/)), Grow a Garden about 21–22M ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)) and Steal a Brainrot about 20–24M ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). Every one is built on survival nights, growing and collecting, or steal-and-defend, which are loops that run for weeks. Direct clones of one-session Steam party games have never reached that tier in the record.

**Roblox's June 2026 discovery overhaul makes the long loop mandatory, not optional.** Roblox said that overvaluing short-term engagement had favored "exciting thumbnails" over long-term value. It rebuilt Recommended For You around retention and split its old qualified play-through signal into separate signals for play-through, session quality and spend ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox)). The retention window grew from **7 days to 28 days, split into D1, D2–7 and D8–28**, starting around June 15 ([ZehnStudio26](https://zehn-studio26.com/news/recommended-for-you-retention-update/)). A DevForum post later added a "deep play-through rate" signal ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-we-are-improving-home-this-year/4502571)). Secondary sources say a **60-second first-play bounce rate** now counts, but Roblox's own wording doesn't confirm it ([Sozai](https://sozai.app/transcript/roblox-algorithm-changes-june-2026/)). Management told investors that discovery had been weighted toward monetization and that the shift could soften monetization in the near term ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/rblxs-discovery-shift-targets-retention-142600721.html)). Some developers reported 70–80% drops in Home impressions during the April test (unconfirmed) ([Endsights](https://endsights.com/roblox-optimizing-discovery-how-great-games-reach-millions-of-players-on-roblox)). For a crossover game, a strong viral hook buys a first session. The algorithm only keeps recommending the game if players come back in week four. That is why every pitch above has a collection, base or progression layer, and why a clickbait thumbnail that promises the original game is now self-defeating. Roblox staff warn that such thumbnails "lead to quick bounces" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/5-tips-from-roblox-staff-to-get-the-most-out-of-thumbnail-personalization/3471689)).

**Four things cap crossover games: saturation, backlash, decay and thin margins.** Every trend draws 7–26 lookalikes, so a clone with no twist splits a shrinking pie. When a developer with an audience objects, the damage is fast. Aggro Crab said it would "rather you pirate our game than play this microtransaction-riddled @Roblox slop ripoff" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/peak-dev-would-rather-you-pirate-peak-than-play-a-microtransaction-roblox-slop-ripoff/)), and CLIFF was renamed RISE with new graphics, or shut down, depending on the report ([Bounding Into Comics](https://boundingintocomics.com/video-games/video-game-news/peak-developers-call-roblox-game-rise-formerly-cliff-a-slop-ripoff-and-encourage-players-to-pirate-our-game-instead); [Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/news/peak-dev-calls-out-roblox-slop-ripoff-cliff-clone-shuts-down/)). Trends decay steeply too. Grow a Garden fell from about $12M in monthly revenue in May 2025 ([Games.gg](https://games.gg/news/roblox-top-earning-games-in-june-2025/)) to a modeled $0.5–1.2M a month in 2026 ([BloxPrices](https://www.bloxprices.com/stats/grow-a-garden/)). The economics are brutally skewed. The top 1,000 Roblox developers averaged about $1.3M in 2025 DevEx payouts ([RoLearn](https://rolearn.dev/insights/developer-payouts-billion-dollar-economy/)), but the median payout among 42,000+ creators was about **$1,500 a year** ([Panstag](https://www.panstag.com/2026/09/roblox-creator-economy-earnings.html)). A small developer should plan for the median and design for the tail.

## Mechanics are free to borrow; names, art and look-and-feel are not

US copyright law protects expression, not rules. *Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive* (D.N.J. 2012) found that Xio's *Mino* infringed. *Mino* copied Tetris's playfield dimensions and piece shapes, and the court rejected the defense that only unprotected rules were taken. It also found trade dress infringement ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc.)). In *Spry Fox v. Lolapps* (2012), the court refused to dismiss Triple Town's suit against the look-alike Yeti Town. That case settled, and Spry Fox took ownership of the clone ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/news/6waves-settles-with-spry-fox-in-yeti-town-triple-town-clone-debate/)). In *DaVinci Editrice v. Ziko Games* (S.D. Tex. 2016), the court found no infringement in a card game with identical mechanics and different art and theme. It held that game rules "do not themselves produce the artistic or literary content that is the hallmark of protectable expression" ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/game-mechanics-arent-protected-copyright-according-texas-court-ruling)). One games-law summary puts the contrast plainly: the Tetris clone "also took the visual aesthetic and was likely infringing," while the Bang! clone "re-skinned the game's rules with a new theme and escaped liability" ([FKKS](https://fkks.com/uploads/news/Games-Law-Summit-Journal_Issue-1_2017.04.27_KANE.pdf)). A legal expert quoted on the Meccha Chameleon clones agreed that similar gameplay alone doesn't establish infringement but copied branding or assets does ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/more-people-are-playing-meccha-chameleon-knock-offs-than-the-original-hit-steam-game-as-the-roblox-clone-machine-goes-into-overdrive-2000715371)). Titles are a separate trademark risk, so clear a name before you commit to it ([Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/intellectual-property-issues-for-game-developers-and-operators-part-1-trademarks-101-names-logos-trade-dress-and-protecting-a-brand-before-launch/)). Two limits apply. These are district-court rulings, persuasive rather than binding nationwide, and the fight in practice is over how abstractly a court describes "the mechanic." A pitch that keeps the original's setting, characters and screen layout invites a narrow, unfavorable description.

**Roblox enforcement is driven by complaints, but it hits hard when it comes.** Roblox's Rules of Conduct forbid using another developer's code, another artist's media, or a brand's logo or designs without permission ([Roblox](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/203313410-Roblox-Rules-of-Conduct)). Its IP guidelines say a notice can target copied expression "even if it has been modified, including by AI tools" ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/ip-guidelines)), which rules out AI-redrawing the original's art. Rights Manager lets rights holders file **even if they have no Roblox presence** ([Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/rights-manager)), so a Steam-only indie can take down your experience. Roblox "only tends to act if a DMCA takedown is filed" ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/how-roblox-theft-is-becoming-a-big-problem)). A valid claim disables the experience and puts a warning on the account. A counter-notice generally restores content in **10–14 business days** unless a lawsuit is filed ([Roblox DMCA guidelines](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/dmca-guidelines)). Repeat-infringer terminations are permanent, and Roblox reserves single-strike termination for egregious willful cases ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/answers-to-your-recent-questions/256614)). Developers also report two notices on one asset counting as two strikes ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/dmca-strikes-stacking-on-the-same-asset/4099893)). In practice the legal risk is rare but heavy-tailed. One claim can wipe out a game's revenue and player base and threaten every other game on the account.

**Licensing is now a real route, and Bombanana! shows how it works.** Roblox launched License Manager in July 2025 with Netflix, Lionsgate, Sega and Kodansha ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/07/roblox-launches-new-licensing-platform-for-experiences)). It went self-serve in November 2025 for "any eligible IP holder… brands with registered IP," typically at a **10–25% revenue share** collected automatically ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/11/roblox-self-serve-ip-licensing)). The "registered IP" wording suggests most small indies aren't listed, so direct contact is the realistic path. That is my inference from the wording. Bombanana! sold over 1M copies in 10 days ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/turkiyes-indie-pc-scene-breaks-out-as-bombanana-sells-one-million-copies-in-10-days/)) and then became the first Steam-native party game with an official Roblox adaptation. FXFX Studio built it "in direct partnership with Lefto Studio and TARK Games" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/steam-hit-bombanana-becomes-first-steam-native-party-game-with-an-official-roblox-adaptation/)), and TARK said it didn't want Roblox to be "just a box to be ticked or a classic port" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=336135)). Terms were not disclosed. Indie developers now have a strong reason to bless one official version: they are cancelling public demos and playtests to avoid AI clones ([Player.One](https://player.one/indie-devs-are-afraid-ai-cloning-their-games-refuse-share-demos-online-164297)). A credible outreach email would offer a revenue share, credit with a link to the Steam page, approval rights for the IP owner, and a redesign built for Roblox. Ask only when you are prepared to build the licensed version. Asking, being told no, and shipping a lookalike anyway is the worst possible look. A properly re-themed original needs no permission, though a short "inspired by" note can earn goodwill. It is not armor, though: CLIFF thanked PEAK's creators in its description and was still attacked ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/how-roblox-theft-is-becoming-a-big-problem)).

## Ship in weeks, but launch something that reads as a genre entry, not a copy

The hardest call is timing. The research is unusually clear about what drives outrage: a near-identical name, a lookalike launched before the original ships, and AI-assisted speed-copying ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/09/17/the-attack-of-the-roblox-clones/); [Gamewave](https://gamewave.fr/roblox/roblox-pille-sans-vergogne-un-jeu-viral-en-developpement/)). Yet the open windows exist *because* most of these originals are unreleased. Worst Class Ever, Find the Needle and Dear Passengers are all slated for Q4 or the end of 2026, and Probably Stolen ships Oct 28. The way through the paradox is distance, not delay. Sneak Squad at a sleepover reads as a new entry in the "hide among NPCs" genre, one Roblox audiences already know from social-deduction games. A classroom-prank game called "Worst School Ever" would read as theft whether it launched before or after Funcore's release. A realistic three-to-five-week build for a small team (my estimate) would land near several of those launch windows anyway. That timing is a bonus: the original's launch press refreshes interest in the genre without your game being the story.

A good sequence starts with a one-day check. Search Roblox for the mechanic's generic keywords, because school-prank and keep-it-up games plausibly exist already under names that web search couldn't surface. Check the Licenses catalog, and run a trademark search on the working title. Then build to two metrics, not to feature parity with the original. The first is the **first 60 seconds**: drop players straight into a round, never into a menu or tutorial wall. The second is a **day-8-to-28 reason to return**, such as an index to complete, a base to upgrade, or a weekly map or event. Live operations are what drive Roblox peaks. Grow a Garden's record of about 22M concurrent players came during an "admin war" event ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)), and Steal An Egg is running its second Halloween part now ([Bloxron](https://bloxron.com/steal-an-egg/stealing)). Plan a weekly update cadence before launch, not after the first spike. Finally, keep the thumbnail honest to your own game. Under the June 2026 signals, a thumbnail that implies the Steam original wins the click and then loses the bounce-rate and retention battle that decides whether Roblox keeps recommending you.

There is a credible opposing view. Clone makers argue that mechanics like wall-punching are common property and that "plagiarism" is an exaggeration ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/)). The punch-wall genre did predate Pandorio by years. That argument is correct about the law on mechanics, and it is why a re-themed original is defensible. It fails on names and timing. Nobody needed to call a game "Escape-the-Wall" one day after Pandorio's reveal unless the point was to capture Pandorio's search traffic. The clone makers won the attention but have no measured audience. The original developer got the sympathy.

## Blocked sources leave these numbers provisional

Every research pass hit the same wall. WebFetch failed on DNS for every site tried, and the egress proxy returned 403 for Roblox (including the DevForum and its APIs), SteamDB, Steam store pages, Reddit, Discord, Rolimons, RoMonitor and most news outlets. **Every figure in this report comes from search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not full reads.** The quality of the numbers varies. Keep It Up!'s 500,000 downloads and Bombanana!'s Roblox visits are publisher-supplied. Next Fest rankings conflict: Worst Class Ever is #1 on one list and #20 on a countdown version. Roblox trackers (RoWatcher, Rolimons, LiveCounts, Statroblox) use different methods and disagree. Revenue figures other than Games.gg's rankings are modeled estimates. The 60-second bounce-rate signal comes from secondary sources, not Roblox's own text.

The most important gap is in the shortlist itself. **"No clone found" means none appeared in web search results.** The Roblox catalog could not be queried, so generic school-prank, balloon or pet-defense games may already exist. Other gaps: no visit or CCU figures exist for any Escape the Walls clone, so the brief's benchmark is unmeasured. Roblox's rules on voice chat for minors and on crime-themed content were not verified. No source tests whether crediting an inspiration reduces takedown or backlash risk. No public takedown of an indie clone with specifics was found. The claim that Pandorio called a clone "a good game" rests on one Japanese report that conflicts with others. Build-time estimates and the fit scores are my judgment. Before committing, re-check four things directly once access allows: the Roblox search results for each pitch's keywords, the live CCU of the leading crossover clones, the original games' release dates, and trademark availability for the chosen title.

## Conclusion

The center of gravity has shifted from copying games to capturing attention. Escape the Walls showed that a single X clip now creates Roblox demand before Steam can sell anything, and that vibe-coded clones can absorb that demand within a day without visibly converting it into lasting players. For a small developer, the defensible position is not being first. It is being the version that is still worth opening on day 28, and the one the original's creator has no reason to call out. That means a mechanic remixed into a Roblox-native long loop, in a world of your own, with the twist the Steam game left out.

A second and more surprising opportunity follows from the same facts. Indie developers are now hiding their prototypes because of clone floods, and Bombanana! proved that an official Roblox version can pull 15M+ visits. Together these create a market for fast, trustworthy Roblox studios that indies can license to. The speed that makes a small team a cloning threat is exactly what an indie developer would pay to have on their side. That partnership pitch may outlast any single trend on this shortlist.
