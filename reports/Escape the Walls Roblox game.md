# One Viral Clip Spawned Roblox's Wall-Punching Clones

"Escape the Walls" did not start on Roblox. It is an **unreleased first-person incremental PC game by London-based Pandorio Games** (Steam app 5450560, release date "Coming soon", no price announced), and the Roblox games that use the name are **copycats that appeared within about 25 hours of the game's viral reveal on X on 14 October 2026** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5450560/Escape_the_Walls/); [Game*Spark via Yahoo! Japan](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/154aad980dc59896f171ad902f8ff34fdbb1cf0a)). The loop is simple: you start in a prison-like cell with only your fists, punch the walls apart block by block, sell the rubble, buy upgrades and dig your way out. A Roblox title called "Escape-the-Wall" went live on 15 October. By 19 October PC Gamer counted **at least seven Roblox clones** ([Quest Board.JP](https://quest-board.jp/en/quests/escape-the-walls/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-steam-next-fest-has-over-8-000-demos-as-worthless-vibe-coded-slop-levels-up/)). The best-documented clone is "Punch Walls to Escape". It made the story bigger because some clone makers openly said they built their games with Anthropic's Claude, and one said "Claude did everything for me, I just sat in my chair and watched" ([Game*Spark on X](https://x.com/gamespark/status/2111011804524793955)). That turned a small indie reveal into one of October 2026's most-cited examples of AI-accelerated cloning. A separate, older and unrelated Roblox project, "Escape The Walls Obby", shares the name but uses a different "outrun the wall" format. The record has one major limit. Every Roblox host and stats tracker was blocked during research, so this report has **no live visit counts, concurrent-player figures, badge lists or gamepass prices for any Roblox version**. Those gaps are flagged throughout.

## Escape the Walls is a Steam game that Roblox copied in a day

The original is a Pandorio Games project. Pandorio is listed on Steam as both developer and publisher ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5450560/Escape_the_Walls/)), is based in London ([Game*Spark via Yahoo! Japan](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/154aad980dc59896f171ad902f8ff34fdbb1cf0a)), and **has mostly made mobile games**. Its other current PC project is the co-op physics game Caravan Bazar, which has a Steam demo ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/)). The studio's public presence is small: an X account, [@pandorioltd](https://x.com/pandorioltd), a Steam page, and a [Discord](https://discord.com/invite/5FesKDMDef) that the X account promotes for sneak peeks and demo news. Pandorio has no known Roblox presence, so any "official" Escape the Walls group or experience on Roblox is almost certainly run by a clone maker.

Pandorio revealed the game under a working title with a gameplay clip on 14 October 2026 ([Pandorio on X](https://x.com/pandorioltd/status/2108549714282983605); [Quest Board.JP](https://quest-board.jp/en/quests/escape-the-walls/)). Reported view counts differ by snapshot and by post. GameRant cites about **2.2M** views on one post. Game*Spark reports about 4.5M by 16 October, Shazoo and Thisisgame report 4.7M, and later X snapshots show about 5.5–5.6M ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/); [Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2026/10/16/173695.html); [Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2026/10/19/192648); [Pandorio on X](https://x.com/pandorioltd/status/2110335569255657628)). The most plausible reading is that the main reveal clip passed 5M views within about a week. The Steam page went live for wishlisting on 18 October. Pandorio reported **more than 500 wishlists in under a day**, a total it said its previous game took about six months to reach ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/); [Pandorio on X](https://x.com/pandorioltd)). That figure comes from the developer and has not been independently verified.

The sources disagree slightly on timing. Some say "the next day", one says "within about a week", and another says "before the Steam page". All three fit a single sequence: the first clone appeared on 15 October and more followed over the next several days. None of the October clones has a confirmed creator name. "Escape-the-Wall" and "Escape the Wall" have no confirmed place ID, and no "Created" or "Updated" timestamps could be retrieved for any of them.

## Punch, vacuum, upgrade: the loop every clone copies

The original's Steam listing describes an incremental game that starts you with only your fists. It says danger hides inside the walls, and that you dig, upgrade and automate, going deeper each time. Reported tags include Incremental, 3D First-Person, 1980s, Atmospheric, Dark, Physics and Singleplayer ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5450560/Escape_the_Walls/)). The economy works like this: you exchange rubble for money and spend it on stronger wall-breaking ([Thisisgame Thailand](https://thisisgamethailand.com/games/console-pc/pc/viral-game-escape-the-walls-copied-on-roblox-less-than-a-day/)). It is an upgrade treadmill, not a round-based survival game. No source describes a fail state, so the natural reading is that you can only be slow, not lose. Pandorio has named no enemies, bosses or specific hazards yet.

The Roblox clones copied this loop and added the usual Roblox features. The clone with the most detail on record is **"Punch Walls to Escape" by Wake it UP** (place 136606353774156). It is free to play and starts you in solitary confinement. You vacuum rubble for cash and climb a tool ladder from **fists to chisels, pickaxes, jackhammers and dynamite**, upgrading the vacuum along the way. It also adds features the single-player original lacks. Up to **four players** can team up in a prison-yard hub. Difficulty "sentences" run from easy to hard, and the hardest unlocks once you escape on Normal. A **Chapter 2** introduces "Big Tony", a huge cellmate who needs a door big enough to fit through ([Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/136606353774156/Punch-Walls-to-Escape)). Other titles in the cluster are documented much more thinly. "Smash Walls To Escape Prison" by BRICKD (place 107557156900608) is a free train-and-smash game. A YouTube video about it was roughly 140 days old when indexed, so it probably **predates** the viral moment and is not a clone ([Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/107557156900608/Smash-Walls-To-Escape-Prison); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GRg0SdA6MQM)). "+1 Punch to Escape" (place 130373433210095) appears in search, but no description of it could be retrieved ([Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/130373433210095/1-Punch-to-Escape)).

| Feature | Original (Pandorio, Steam) | "Punch Walls to Escape" (Roblox) |
|---|---|---|
| Players | Single-player | Up to 4 co-op |
| Currency | Rubble exchanged for money | Rubble vacuumed for cash |
| Progression | Dig, upgrade, automate, go deeper | Fists → chisel → pickaxe → jackhammer → dynamite, plus vacuum upgrades |
| Structure | Repeated runs, "danger hides inside the walls" | Difficulty "sentences", Chapter 2 with Big Tony |
| Price | Unannounced | Free |

Pets, rebirths, auras and skins are documented for **no** Escape the Walls clone. Those systems belong to older Roblox wall-punching simulators. Break Wall Simulator, for example, advertises rebirths, pet trading and a Premium perk of +10% Strength and +10% Punching Speed ([Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/13013415523/Break-Wall-Simulator)). Clones may add these systems later, but nothing on record shows they have.

## Seven clones in five days, some reportedly built with Claude

The controversy is the story. A copy was live less than 25 hours after Pandorio's clip ([Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2026/10/19/192648)). Several clones used near-identical names, such as "Escape the Wall" ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/)). By 19 October PC Gamer counted at least seven on Roblox and at least one on Steam. PC Gamer also reported that a competing studio tweeted its own clone at Pandorio with "Our Steam page is live too!" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-steam-next-fest-has-over-8-000-demos-as-worthless-vibe-coded-slop-levels-up/)). One repost names that studio "Duct Tape Games", but PC Gamer's own text does not, so the attribution is unconfirmed ([7MMO](https://7mmo.com/threads/steam-week-in-review-steam-next-fest-has-over-8-000-demos-as-worthless-vibe-coded-slop-levels-up.8052/)).

The AI angle made the story spread. GameRant, Gamewave and Kotaku en Español all report that **some Roblox creators confirmed using AI tools such as Claude** ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/); [Gamewave](https://gamewave.fr/roblox/roblox-pille-sans-vergogne-un-jeu-viral-en-developpement/); [Kotaku en Español](https://es.kotaku.com/un-juego-indie-se-volvio-viral-antes-de-salir-en-steam-y-roblox-ya-esta-lleno-de-copias-2000055077)). Japanese outlet pixmofu says both clone creators it examined appear to have used Claude ([pixmofu](https://pixmofu.com/articles/escape-the-walls-roblox-clone-claude)). Kotaku placed the case within a wider wave of "vibe-coded" ripoffs, alongside the Needle In A Haystack clone-of-a-clone dispute ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/devs-behind-needle-in-a-haystack-clone-complain-their-clone-has-been-cloned-by-vibe-coder-2000747284)). PC Gamer's headline put it more bluntly: "worthless vibe coded slop levels up." In Japan, X ran a trending topic that roughly translates as "wall-punch escape game video goes viral, clones flood in immediately, exposing the reality of AI imitation" ([X Trending](https://x.com/i/trending/2111323087748882493)). The episode was then cited in wider coverage of indie studios pulling public tests. Moonlighter's studio cancelled a public playtest over AI-led cloning ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/moonlighter-studio-wont-host-a-public-playtest-of-its-next-game-due-to-rampant-ai-led-cloning-we-know-were-not-the-only-developers-feeling-this-way/)), and GamingHQ and GameBusiness.jp reported similar retreats ([GamingHQ](https://gaminghq.eu/2026/10/18/indie-developers-roblox-copycat-games/); [GameBusiness.jp](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/10/21/28399.html)).

Reception split into identifiable camps. The majority sided with Pandorio and accused the clone makers of theft ([Quest Board.JP](https://quest-board.jp/en/quests/escape-the-walls/)). A fatalist camp, reflected in Game*Spark's headline about voices saying "ideas shouldn't be made public", blamed indie developers for showing work early. One reply under the reveal predicted the whole episode: "you have exactly 7 hours before someone makes this on roblox and gets 20x your revenue" ([AaliyanRBLX on X](https://x.com/AaliyanRBLX/status/2110458012205011029)). Another said: "All you're doing is giving some 10 year old on roblox an idea to steal" ([ZanderDSOW on X](https://x.com/ZanderDSOW/status/2110510744148013167)). Some players simply wanted it on Roblox ([altulige on X](https://x.com/altulige/status/2110934869698019542)). The clone makers argued that punching walls is a common idea and that calling it plagiarism is an exaggeration ([Kotaku en Español](https://es.kotaku.com/un-juego-indie-se-volvio-viral-antes-de-salir-en-steam-y-roblox-ya-esta-lleno-de-copias-2000055077)). That defence holds for the mechanic but not for the name. Copying the wall-punching loop has years of Roblox precedent, but choosing titles nearly identical to a game that went viral the day before is clearly trading on its attention. English outlets report that **Pandorio has made no official comment** ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/)). Only pixmofu claims the developer replied that a clone was "a good game", and that claim remains unconfirmed ([pixmofu](https://pixmofu.com/articles/escape-the-walls-roblox-clone-claude)). No source reports Roblox removing any clone, and no source documents scams or exploits tied to these experiences.

## Roblox had a wall-punching genre long before October 2026

The clone makers have a point: the mechanic already had a large Roblox audience. One widely shared rebuttal noted that **Punch Wall Simulator has been on Roblox since March 2023 with over 150M visits, and Wall Punch Simulator has about 15M more**. It also pointed out that "there is even a Roblox obby already called Escape The Walls" ([Ruben on X](https://x.com/rdominguezibar/status/2110794822550474870)). These figures come from a social post and could not be checked against Roblox. Even so, the long-running YouTube niche of "Breaking 931,712,864 Walls With a Punch" videos supports the general point ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W37K_6C3Ayk)). The October clones are best understood as a hybrid. They take Pandorio's prison-escape framing and name and attach them to an existing Roblox punch-and-upgrade simulator template, which made them very easy to build quickly.

The older namesakes are a separate lineage. **"Escape The Walls Obby [ALPHA]"**, documented in a September 2025 DevForum devlog, combines randomly generated obbies with the classic "Be Crushed By A Speeding Wall" format. Players outrun a moving wall rather than punch through one. The developer enlarged the walls from 40x40 to 100x100 studs and added a portal hub because players quit after the first obby. Nearly every stage got a branch to a safe zone. A confusing "Tix" currency gate was replaced by a boulder that blocks progress between obby groups and is cleared by eating chicken at a deli ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/makin-it-escape-the-walls-obby-devlog-1/3930017)). The developer also floated jetpacks, speed coils and invisibility cloaks as power-ups ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/looking-for-thoughts-on-my-game/3920187)). A second older title, "ESCAPE the WALL Obby!!" by The Wall Experience (place 18873717199), has a listing that is mostly premium-perk promotion. Its place ID suggests a creation date around mid-2024, but that is inferred from the number, not confirmed ([Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/18873717199/ESCAPE-the-WALL-Obby)). Neither obby has any connection to Pandorio. There is also a Google Play app called "Escape the Walls" under the package `com.anintellectualcompany.escapethewalls`. Its connection to Pandorio or to any Roblox clone is unverified, and the package name does not match Pandorio ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.anintellectualcompany.escapethewalls)).

## Badges, gamepasses and player counts remain blank

The research could not fill in several things the brief asked for. The proxy blocked roblox.com, games.roblox.com, apis.roblox.com, badges.roblox.com and the stats trackers (Rolimon's, RoMonitor, RoTrends). Most news pages could only be read through search summaries, not full articles. The gaps are therefore a measurement failure, not proof that the data does not exist. They are also partly real. The clones are only one to two weeks old, and searches for codes returned only unrelated games such as The Escape and Escape the Cave ([RobloxDen](https://robloxden.com/game-codes/the-escape)). Several outlets said explicitly that they found no visit figures for any clone ([OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/37989/viral-wall-punching-game-already-has-roblox-clones); [Ralify](https://ralify.net/stories/viral-indie-game-escape-the-walls-faces-immediate-roblox-copycat/)).

## Conclusion

The search term itself has been captured. A player who types "Escape the Walls" into Roblox today finds games that did not exist before 15 October, while the game that created the demand has no release date and no price. Ralify's advice to verify the developer through the Steam page rather than play the first Roblox result shows how upside-down the situation is ([Ralify](https://ralify.net/stories/viral-indie-game-escape-the-walls-faces-immediate-roblox-copycat/)). The episode shows the gap between a developer's public reveal and a clone shrinking to about a day. What made it fast was not only AI tools but also an existing Roblox wall-punching simulator template that a viral premise could be attached to almost immediately. The most valuable thing the original had was attention, not its mechanics, and the clones took that attention first.

For anyone covering the topic, the evidence points to the clone race, not to any single Roblox game, as the subject. Two questions will decide how the story ends, and neither can be answered yet. The first is whether any clone converts the borrowed attention into lasting players, which needs the Roblox statistics this research could not reach. The second is whether Pandorio's 500-wishlist first day survives a launch window in which Roblox already offers free co-op versions with chapters the original has not shipped. Re-checking Roblox visit counts and the Steam page once access allows is the obvious next step.
